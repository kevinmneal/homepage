A bit about me

I trained as an evolutionary biologist and moved into data science and then applied AI engineering after my PhD. I enjoy the work, but I never lost my interest in open-ended research. Building a career and raising a family left little time to pursue it on my own.

Recent reasoning models, paired with agents that can run code, inspect results, and keep working through a problem, have changed what I can take on.

What first caught my attention was the ambitious games people were building with AI. I made one too: MAKE WAY!, a medieval physics playground. Then I started wondering: could these tools do fresh work with my old PhD data?

Seeing AI-assisted mathematical research and formal proofs encouraged me to push further. That curiosity has brought me back to spadefoot biogeography and, unexpectedly, into computational geometry.

That’s what draws me to agentic science: opening up research I couldn’t otherwise find time for, while keeping evidence, uncertainty, reproducibility, and independent criticism at the center of the work.
